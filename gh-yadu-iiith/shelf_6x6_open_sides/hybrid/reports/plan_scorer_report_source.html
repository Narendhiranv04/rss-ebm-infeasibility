Per-step plan scorer: what we changed and what we found

Robot obstacle relocation, shelf scenes (xArm7, side grasps). Status as of 23 Nov 2026. A fresh test set is being generated; its runs are marked.

Summary
  • One part of the team's V3.4 pipeline was replaced: the model that scores candidate plans. The candidate plans are V3.4's own, so every difference comes from re-scoring the same plans.
  • Pre-registered test, run once. On 3D TEST the new scorer solves 90.5% of task scenes, against 80.8% for the current scorer (XYNet), and passes the pre-set bar. In 2D it is far ahead on task but failed the bar on "give up".
  • Compared at the same give-up rate, the new scorer solves about 10 more task points in 3D and about 25 more in 2D. So the 2D miss comes from the default threshold, not a weaker model (exploratory).
  • Where the gain comes from (3D VAL): scoring each step separately, instead of the whole plan, gives the task gain; about a third of it needs our encoder. The "which step failed" labels do not change task success; they add about 5 points on "do nothing".
  • Against the classical planner E-M4M on 3D TEST (exploratory): +20.3 points on task at E-M4M's 300 CPU-second limit and +14.5 at 600 s, but 3.5 points worse on "do nothing" and 9.7 worse on "give up". About 0.1 s per scene against a median 109 CPU-seconds. Timed on one computer, the new scorer takes about as long as XYNet.
  • Position noise in what the planner sees (sigma 5 to 20 mm) costs the new scorer 1 to 3 task points, and the lead over XYNet stays (3D VAL). Retraining on noisy labels shows no detectable gain.
  • Limits: simulation only, up to 3 moves, the chosen plans need angled approaches (with straight-in grasps only about 9% still work), and a new setup needs a short recalibration.

1. The problem in one paragraph

The robot has to pick a target object from a crowded 6×6 shelf grid. Other objects are in the way. It can only grasp from the side, because there is a shelf above. So it has to decide which blockers to move, in what order, and where to put them. Sometimes nothing needs to move. Sometimes the task is impossible and the right answer is to give up. A physics simulator (the "teacher") checks whether a plan actually works.

2. What we changed, and why

The team's pipeline has four parts:

  1. cVAE suggests a few places to put each blocker (4 per object).
  2. Enumeration lists every plan of 1 to 3 moves from those suggestions.
  3. Scorer gives each plan a probability of success.
  4. Rule picks: do the best plan, do nothing, or give up (thresholds: noop gate 0.5, eta 0.3).

Only part 3 was replaced. Multiplying per-step success is not new on its own (STAP, Text2Motion), and scoring whole plans jointly is the PIGINet approach. What is tested here is the difference between the two on identical candidate plans, with per-step training labels from the physics teacher.

Old scorer: XYNetNew scorer: per-step hazard model
Looks at the whole plan at once and outputs one number.Scores each step on its own ("will this move work, given the shelf right now?") and multiplies the steps together.
Trained on "did the whole plan work, yes or no".Also trained on which step failed, which the teacher already tells us.

Why per-step scoring helps. If move 1 is impossible, the plan fails however good moves 2 and 3 are. Multiplying step scores makes one bad step pull the whole plan down. A single whole-plan score can miss this.

The bar. Fixed before TEST was scored, the new scorer passes only if both hold:

  • Task: it is better than XYNet. Formally, the lower bound of the difference is above 0.
  • Do nothing and give up: it is not more than 10 points worse than XYNet. Formally, the lower bound of each difference is above −10.

The lower bound is the most pessimistic difference still consistent with the data (97.5% one-sided). It comes from resampling whole scene families 10,000 times, so related scenes are not counted as independent evidence. Task gets a strict bar because it is the main claim. The other two only need to show no serious loss.

Why 2D failed. Only one check missed: give up. On impossible 2D scenes the new scorer gave up 84.2% of the time against XYNet's 92.0%, a gap of −7.8 points. Allowing for chance, the lower bound was −12.0, just past the −10 allowance. The cause is the default give-up threshold (eta 0.3). On some impossible 2D scenes the new scorer rates a plan just above 0.3, so it tries the plan instead of giving up. At a higher eta it gives up as often as XYNet and still solves far more task scenes (section 5). That fix was found after TEST was used, so it is exploratory and does not change the pre-registered verdict.

3D is the main study and 2D was for ablations.

4. Why it works exploratory

It avoids plans whose first move is impossible

On 3D TEST (723 runs each), XYNet picked a plan where the first object could not be grasped 47 times. The new scorer did it 8 times. Total physics failures: 51 for the new scorer, 107 for XYNet.

Where the gain comes from

Concern: the gain could come from a better scene encoder rather than from per-step scoring. Check: the per-step training was kept and the encoder swapped for several others, including XYNet's own (3D VAL, seed 0).

Every row with per-step training has a 3-seed mean task success between 87 and 92 (single seeds 85.5 to 93.2). The whole-plan PIGINet-style model is about 20 points lower, and its "give up" swings from 10 to 62 across seeds.

Concern: the PIGINet-style model may only look weak because its thresholds were never tuned. Check: both models were given the same threshold tuning on VAL (37 candidate rules, same selection criterion). Tuned, PIGINet-style reaches 67 to 71 on task against 88 to 92 for ours, so tuning does not close the gap. Both were tuned and scored on VAL, so both numbers are optimistic by the same amount.

Without the "which step failed" labels, task success is the same, but "do nothing" drops by 5.1 points, and it is lower on all three seeds (−9.4, −3.5, −2.4). "Give up" moves in both directions across seeds. The step labels mainly help on scenes where nothing should be moved.

Reading the table: scoring each step separately gives the task gain; about a third of it needs our encoder; the step labels add about 5 points on "do nothing" only. XYNet's three seeds differ by about 1 point on task (78.2 to 79.1). The PIGINet-style model is our own reimplementation, trained with the same data and budget as ours.

How good the ranking is, and whether more candidates help

Concern: success could be limited by the candidate plans rather than by the scorer. Check 1: on task scenes, always act on the top-ranked plan (no thresholds) and see how often a working plan sits near the top.

A working plan is almost always among the new scorer's top 3. This is an upper bound for "check the top 3 plans in simulation before acting": it assumes the check agrees with the physics teacher every time, covers task scenes only, and each check costs about 15 s (TEST is post-hoc here).

Check 2: give the cVAE 6 or 8 suggested spots per blocker instead of 4 (3D VAL). Thresholds stay at their 4-spot values, and all three use fresh CPU candidate pools. Task success does not rise (new scorer 92.3, 91.4, 90.0 for 4, 6, 8 spots; XYNet 77.7, 77.7, 78.6), and "give up" falls for the new scorer (98.3, 96.6, 91.4). A likely reason, not tested, is that more candidates give more chances to rate a bad plan highly. With these thresholds, more spots do not help.

5. Threshold choice exploratory · TEST

Concern: the gain could come from better-placed thresholds rather than a better model. Check: the give-up threshold (eta) was swept over its full range for both models, and task success plotted against give-up accuracy. Up and to the right is better.

The 2D miss comes from where the default threshold sits, not from a weaker model: at equal give-up accuracy, the new scorer solves about 25 points more task scenes. With eta chosen on VAL, both 2D and 3D pass the bar on TEST. This is exploratory, since TEST had already been used.

In 2D, the default rule (eta 0.3) acts on 65 of 450 impossible-scene runs on TEST (3 seeds pooled). Raising eta to 0.5 cuts that to 27 while losing only 2 of 919 task successes; VAL shows the same pattern (73 to 39, and 937 to 936). The eta sweep on VAL picks 0.6, and at that value 2D TEST gives task 74.1 against XYNet 50.5 and give up 94.7 against 96.2 (lower bound −2.9).

Calibration. When the model says "80% chance", the plan should work about 80% of the time. Average gap on 3D TEST: 6.4 points for the new scorer, 9.8 for XYNet (2D: 18 vs 29). No confidence interval was computed for these gaps. A cutoff chosen to keep the failure rate under 10% (95% upper bound) also kept it under 10% on TEST, and the new scorer still acts on 98.6% of scenes. For XYNet no cutoff meets that bound.

Concern: tuned thresholds may be overfitted to these scenes. Check: a threshold tuned on 3D and applied to 2D gave 27% failures instead of 10%, so thresholds do not transfer between setups. The headline result uses the team's default rule, and a new setup gets recalibrated on a small labelled set.

8. Robustness exploratory · 3D TEST plans

Both models' chosen plans were re-checked in the simulator with small changes, as a stand-in for the real world being slightly different.

  • Friction and mass changes barely matter. One run per setting, so gaps of 1 to 2 points are noise.
  • About 90% of the plans that fail under position shifts fail with "no feasible side grasp": a shifted neighbour closes the gap the fingers need. Failures grow with plan length.
  • With the G2 gripper model, 91.5% vs 83.1% of all chosen plans still work (from 92.8% vs 85.7%).
  • With straight-in grasps only, about 9 to 10% still work for both models: the plans rely on angled approaches. A rig limited to straight-in grasps needs new scenes.

  • One run per shift (hardware/robust/runs_server.jsonl). The new scorer stays 6 to 11 points ahead at every shift. Up to 5 mm it loses under 3 points and XYNet about 6; at 10 mm both lose about 10. Cameras accurate to about 5 mm look sufficient (estimate, simulation only).
  • Grasp check before acting (3D VAL): before moving, check in the simulator that each object of the top plan can still be grasped, and fall back to the next of the top 5 plans if not. With 5 mm shifts success rises from 88.1% to 93.2%, with 10 mm from 84.9% to 90.4%, and no plan that worked was lost; on 10 (5 mm) and 18 (10 mm) of 219 scenes none of the 5 plans passed. Each check takes about 15 s. The check runs in the same simulator, on the shifted scene, as the success test, so it assumes the robot sees the true positions; seed 0 only.
  • The planner sees wrong positions (3D VAL): positions given to the models get Gaussian noise (sigma 5, 10, 20 mm, kept inside the object's cell), while the physics check uses the true scene. Task success: new scorer 92.3, 91.4, 90.5, 89.5 at sigma 0, 5, 10, 20 mm; XYNet 77.7, 79.1, 78.6, 75.9. "Do nothing" and "give up" barely move. Seed 0 only. The new scorer loses 1 to 3 points; XYNet is flat within noise.
  • Training on noisy labels (3D VAL): three retrained versions (labels from shifted scenes only, shifted plus normal, and a control with the same step information but no shifts), three seeds each. Under 5 and 10 mm shifts none differs from the current model by more than 1.6 points, and every 95% interval includes zero. No gain is detectable; gains up to about 3 points cannot be ruled out.
  • Moving the board 10 cm lower halves success for both. Neither model sees the board height, so neither can adapt. A short calibration in each new setup is needed; giving the model the geometry as input is future work.
